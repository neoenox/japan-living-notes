# Moving In: Utilities & Setup in Japan
### A Practical Guide for Foreign Residents: From Key Day to Your First 30 Days

*Edition: 2026. Procedures differ by city, utility company, and building. Always confirm the details with your management company and each provider.*

---

## Disclaimer

This guide is general information based on public sources and common practice. It is not legal advice. Rules, deadlines, and fees change and differ by city and company. Check the official sources listed at the end before you act.

---

## Chapter 0: Quick Start

You signed the lease. Now you need electricity, water, gas, internet, a registered address, and a working daily routine. None of it is hard, but three steps have deadlines, and one of them (gas) needs you to be at home.

**Your first 30 days on one page**

1. **2 weeks before moving in:** book gas opening, apply for electricity and water, order internet.
2. **Key day:** photograph every scratch and stain, check the air conditioner and appliances, email the photos to the management company.
3. **Move-in day:** be at home for the gas visit. Switch on the breaker.
4. **Within 14 days:** report your new address at the city or ward office, and register for National Health Insurance if you need it.
5. **Week 1-2:** send a change-of-address notice to Japan Post, update your bank, phone, employer, and school.
6. **Week 2-4:** learn the garbage rules, meet the management company's contact, and finish internet installation.

**Total time:** most of the work takes a few hours spread over two weeks. Start the bookings before you move, because busy seasons (March and April) fill up quickly.

## Chapter 1: Before the Move

### 1.1 Two weeks before

- **Gas opening (開栓, kaisen):** book the visit. Someone must be at home, and it is easier to get your preferred time if you book 1-2 weeks ahead. In March and April, book even earlier.
- **Electricity (電気):** apply online or by phone. You usually do not need to be present.
- **Water (水道):** apply to the city waterworks bureau. You usually do not need to be present. In some buildings the building as a whole has a water contract, so ask the management company first.
- **Internet:** order now. Installation can take 1-4 weeks, and some buildings need permission for work.
- **Moving company or delivery:** get at least two quotes, and ask what is included.

### 1.2 What to ask the management company

- Which utilities do I apply for myself, and which are already set up?
- Which electricity and gas companies serve the building?
- Is the internet line already in the building? Which providers are allowed?
- Where are the meters, the breaker, and the garbage station?
- Who do I call if something breaks, and how?

### 1.3 Key day

On the day you receive the keys:

- Photograph **every** scratch, stain, and damaged spot, and email the photos to the management company. This protects your deposit later.
- Check the air conditioner, water heater, intercom, locks, and lights.
- Count the keys you received, and ask what to do with spare keys.
- Check that the breaker works and the water runs.

## Chapter 2: Utilities, One by One

### 2.1 Electricity

- **How to start:** apply to an electricity company by phone or online. Give your new address, move-in date, and contact information. A visit is usually not needed.
- **Move-in day:** switch on the breaker (ブレーカー). If a smart meter is installed, power often starts by itself after the application date.
- **Choosing a company:** the local company works, and you can change later. Compare the basic charge and the unit price, not only the headline discount.
- **Amperage (アンペア):** a higher amperage allows more appliances at once but can raise the basic fee in some plans. Ask the management company or the provider about the suitable size.

### 2.2 Water

- **How to start:** apply to the waterworks bureau of your new address by phone, online, or by postcard left in the apartment. A visit is usually not needed.
- **Billing:** many cities bill every two months, together with sewerage. Some buildings include water in the management fee.

### 2.3 Gas (the one that needs you)

- **Two types:** city gas (都市ガス) and propane gas (プロパン, LP gas). They use different companies and prices, so ask which one the building has.
- **Opening (開栓):** a worker comes to open the gas and check the appliances. **You or a representative must be at home.** It takes roughly 10-20 minutes.
- **Booking:** apply by website or phone. Book 1-2 weeks before moving in. Busy seasons fill up.
- **Choose a time** after your main moving work is done, so you can be at home calmly.
- **Closing (閉栓)** when you move out usually does not need you to be present.

### 2.4 Internet

- **Fiber (光回線):** fast and stable. Installation can take 1-4 weeks and sometimes needs a visit and building permission.
- **Alternatives for the first weeks:** a pocket Wi-Fi or a phone hotspot.
- **Before ordering:** ask the management company whether the building already has a line, and which providers are allowed.
- **Watch the contract:** check the minimum contract period, the cancellation fee, and the monthly cost after promotional periods.

### 2.5 Bills and payment

| Item | Usual payment | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Electricity, gas, water | Credit card, bank transfer, or convenience-store payment | Choose automatic payment to avoid missing a bill |
| Internet | Credit card or bank transfer | Check the contract period |
| Rent and management fee | Usually bank transfer | Check the exact date and method in your contract |

## Chapter 3: Official Registration (Within 14 Days)

### 3.1 The address report

When you move, you must report your new address to the city or ward office.

- **Moving between municipalities:** a move-out notice (転出届, tenshutsu todoke) at your old city office, and a move-in notice (転入届, tennyu todoke) at the new one.
- **Moving within the same municipality:** a change-of-address notice (転居届, tenkyo todoke).
- **Deadline:** **within 14 days** of the day you start living at the new address. A late report is possible, but a fine can apply in principle.
- **Bring:** your residence card (在留カード). Bring your My Number card and your passport if you have them. Some offices ask for all household members' cards.
- **Residence card:** the address on the back is updated at the same visit. You do not need to go to the immigration office.

### 3.2 National Health Insurance and pension

- If you do not receive health insurance through your employer, you must register for **National Health Insurance** (国民健康保険) at the same office. The usual deadline is also **14 days**. A late registration can mean back payments or full payment of medical bills.
- **Pension (年金):** employees are enrolled through their employer. Others should ask the office about the National Pension (国民年金).
- **Employer or school:** tell them your new address and bring any required proof.

### 3.3 My Number card

If you have a My Number card, the address on it is updated at the same visit. Some offices ask for each family member's card and PIN.

### 3.4 Mail forwarding

Japan Post's **e-Tenkyo** (e転居) service forwards mail from your old address to the new one for one year, free of charge. Registration takes a few business days, and you need a Yu-ID with identity verification. You can renew once for a further year.

### 3.5 What to bring on registration day

☐ Residence card
☐ Passport
☐ My Number card (if you have one)
☐ Lease contract or proof of address (some offices ask)
☐ Seal or signature, if requested
☐ Health insurance card, if moving with your employer's insurance

## Chapter 4: Money, Phone and Internet

### 4.1 Updating your bank

- Update your address with your bank, usually online, by app, or at a branch.
- If you do not have an account yet: some banks require several months of residence or a certain remaining stay period. Ask the bank before you go, and bring your residence card and passport.
- Japan Post Bank is a common first choice, but the requirements differ by branch and over time, so confirm them.

### 4.2 Mobile phones

- To sign a contract you usually need a residence card. Many carriers also ask for a credit card or a bank account for payment.
- A short remaining stay period can limit your options. Ask in advance.
- **SIM-only and low-cost plans** are often cheaper than the big carriers, but have fewer shops.

### 4.3 Seals and signatures

Many procedures now accept a signature. Some banks and offices still ask for a seal (印鑑). Ask before you buy one, and keep a cheap seal if one is needed.

### 4.4 Home insurance

Your fire insurance for the apartment is usually decided at the lease signing. Check what it covers, the contact phone, and how to claim.

## Chapter 5: Garbage, Noise and Neighbors

### 5.1 Garbage

Garbage rules differ by city and sometimes by building. Typical categories:

| Category | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Burnable (燃えるごみ) | Food waste, paper, small items | Collection days vary by city |
| Non-burnable (燃えないごみ) | Small metal, glass, ceramics | Collection days vary by city |
| Recyclables | PET bottles, cans, bottles, paper | Rinse and separate caps and labels |
| Bulky waste (粗大ごみ) | Furniture, large appliances | Book in advance and pay a fee |

- Ask the management company for the **schedule, the correct bags, and the station location**.
- Put garbage out **on the right morning**, usually by a set time, not the night before.
- Large electric appliances (TV, washing machine, air conditioner, refrigerator) usually need a special collection. Ask the shop or your city.

### 5.2 Noise and neighbors

- Keep noise low in the evening and at night, especially in apartments with thin walls.
- A short greeting to the neighbors is welcome but not required in most city buildings.
- Do not smoke on the balcony unless it is allowed.
- Complaints about garbage and noise are common, so follow the building rules.

### 5.3 Building rules

Read the management rules: bicycle parking, balcony use, deliveries, package lockers, and quiet hours.

## Chapter 6: When Something Breaks

### 6.1 Who to call

- Call the **management company** (管理会社) first, not the landlord. Ask in writing when possible, and keep your messages.
- Typical urgent problems: water leaks, no hot water, power failure, locked out of the apartment.

### 6.2 Who pays

- Repairs of parts that came with the apartment are usually the landlord's responsibility, unless you caused the damage.
- Small consumables (light bulbs, batteries, filters) are usually yours.
- Check the repair section of your contract before you call a repair company yourself.

### 6.3 Emergency numbers

- **110:** police
- **119:** fire and ambulance
- Gas smell: stop using fire, open windows, and call the gas company's emergency line.

### 6.4 Keep records

Photograph the problem, write the date, and send it to the management company. Keep the replies.

## Chapter 7: Your First 30 Days

A simple order that works for most people:

**Before moving in**

1. Book gas opening.
2. Apply for electricity and water.
3. Order internet.
4. Arrange the moving truck or delivery.

**Key day and move-in day**

5. Photograph existing damage and email it.
6. Check appliances and the air conditioner.
7. Switch on the breaker.
8. Be at home for the gas visit.

**Within 14 days**

9. Report your address at the city or ward office.
10. Register for National Health Insurance if needed.
11. Update your residence card address (done at the same visit).

**Weeks 2-4**

12. Mail forwarding with Japan Post (e-Tenkyo).
13. Update your bank, phone, employer, and school.
14. Learn the garbage rules and the building rules.
15. Finish internet installation.

*Tip: Send in English. Paste the Japanese version if you can have it checked. Keep sentences short.*

**M1. Gas opening booking**

> Subject: Gas opening request: [Address, Room number]
>
> Hello,
> I will move into [Address, Room number] on [date]. I would like to book a gas opening visit on [date and time 1] or [date and time 2].
> My name is [Name]. My phone number is [Phone]. I will be at home for the visit.
> Thank you.

**M2. Water start**

> Subject: Request to start water service: [Address, Room number]
>
> Hello,
> I will move into [Address, Room number] on [date]. I would like to start water service on that day.
> My name: [Name] / Phone: [Phone] / Email: [Email]
> Please tell me if you need anything else. Thank you.

**M3. Report a problem to the management company**

> Subject: Problem in room [Room number]: [short description]
>
> Hello,
> I live in [Property name, Room number]. [Describe the problem briefly: what, since when.]
> I have attached photos. Could you please tell me who will visit and when?
> Thank you.
> [Name / Phone]

**M4. Reschedule internet installation**

> Subject: Request to change installation date: [Order number]
>
> Hello,
> My internet installation is scheduled for [date and time]. I would like to change it to [date and time 1] or [date and time 2].
> Please let me know if that is possible. Thank you.
> [Name / Phone / Address]

**M5. Ask about garbage rules**

> Subject: Question about garbage rules: [Property name]
>
> Hello,
> I moved into [Property name, Room number] on [date]. Could you please tell me the garbage collection schedule, the correct bags, and where to put them?
> Thank you.
> [Name / Room number]
