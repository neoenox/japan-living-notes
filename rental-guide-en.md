# Renting an Apartment in Japan
### A Practical Guide for Foreign Residents: Costs, Contracts, Emails, and Move-in

*Edition: 2026. Figures are typical ranges, not fixed rules. Always confirm the details with your real estate agent.*

---

## Disclaimer

This guide is general information based on public sources and common practice. It is not legal advice. Rules, fees, and practices change and differ by city, landlord, and agency. Check the official sources listed at the end and your own contract before you pay or sign anything.

---

## Chapter 0: Quick Start

Renting in Japan feels difficult for three reasons: the upfront cost is high, the process has unfamiliar steps, and some landlords hesitate to rent to foreigners. All three are manageable once you know what to expect.

**The whole process on one page**

1. **Set your budget and conditions** (1 week). Decide rent, area, and commute.
2. **Contact agencies** (1 week). Use an agency that accepts foreign customers, and send the inquiry email in Appendix C.
3. **View properties** (1-2 weeks). Use the viewing checklist in Appendix D.
4. **Apply** (1 day). Submit the application form and documents.
5. **Screening** (2-7 days). The landlord and the guarantor company review you.
6. **Explanation of important matters and contract** (1-2 days). Read carefully, ask questions, then sign.
7. **Pay initial costs** (before move-in). Usually by bank transfer.
8. **Get the keys and move in.** Set up utilities and register your address.

**Total time:** usually 3-6 weeks from first inquiry to keys. Most properties will not wait more than 1-2 months for you, so start searching about 1-2 months before you need to move.

**Typical upfront cost:** about **4 to 6 times the monthly rent**. An ¥80,000 apartment can mean roughly ¥320,000-¥480,000 before you move in. Chapter 2 explains each item, and the Excel calculator in your download calculates it for you.

## Chapter 1: How Renting Works in Japan

### 1.1 Who is who

- **Landlord (大家, ooya):** owns the building.
- **Management company (管理会社, kanri gaisha):** handles repairs, rent collection, and complaints after you move in. Often this is who you call, not the landlord.
- **Real estate agency (不動産会社, fudosan gaisha):** shows you properties and handles the paperwork. Some agencies only introduce properties; others also manage them.
- **Guarantor company (保証会社, hosho gaisha):** a company that guarantees your rent to the landlord for a fee. Most landlords now require one.

### 1.2 Floor plan codes

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1R | One room, kitchen inside the room |
| 1K | One room plus a separate kitchen |
| 1DK | One room plus dining-kitchen |
| 1LDK | One room plus living-dining-kitchen |
| 2LDK | Two rooms plus living-dining-kitchen |

L = living, D = dining, K = kitchen, R = room.

### 1.3 Room size

Size is given in square meters (㎡) and sometimes in tatami mats (畳, jo). One tatami is roughly 1.5-1.65 ㎡, but the size varies by region, so check the ㎡ figure. A typical 1K is about 20-25 ㎡.

### 1.4 Building type and age

- **Mansion (マンション):** reinforced concrete. Better sound insulation.
- **Apartment (アパート):** usually wood or light steel frame. Cheaper, but sound travels more.
- **Age (築年数, chikunensu):** older buildings are cheaper. Buildings completed after 1981 follow stricter earthquake standards, so ask about the year of completion.

### 1.5 Two kinds of contracts

- **Ordinary lease (普通借家契約):** the standard. Usually 2 years, renewable. The tenant is strongly protected.
- **Fixed-term lease (定期借家契約):** ends on a fixed date and may not renew. Rent is often lower. Be careful if you want to stay long.

Always check which one you are signing.

## Chapter 2: Know Your Costs

### 2.1 Initial costs (typical)

| Item | Japanese | Typical amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| First month's rent | 前家賃 | 1 month | Often prorated if you move in mid-month |
| Security deposit | 敷金 (shikikin) | 0-2 months | Partly returned at move-out after repair costs |
| Key money | 礼金 (reikin) | 0-2 months | A gift to the landlord. **Not returned.** |
| Agency fee | 仲介手数料 | Up to 0.5-1 month + tax | The legal cap for landlord and tenant combined is 1 month + tax. Your default share is 0.5 month + tax; you pay up to 1 month only if you agreed in advance |
| Guarantor company fee | 保証料 | 50-100% of 1 month | Sometimes plus annual renewal fee |
| Fire insurance | 火災保険 | About ¥15,000-20,000 for 2 years | Usually required |
| Key exchange | 鍵交換費用 | About ¥10,000-25,000 | Sometimes required |
| Management/common fee | 管理費・共益費 | Monthly | Paid every month with rent |
| Other | Cleaning, support packages | Varies | **Ask what each is for** |

### 2.2 Monthly costs

Rent + management fee + utilities (electricity, gas, water) + internet + parking if needed. Compare apartments by **rent + management fee**, not rent alone.

### 2.3 Ongoing costs you may forget

- **Renewal fee (更新料):** common in some regions (e.g., Tokyo area), often 1 month's rent every 2 years. Not all properties charge it.
- **Guarantor renewal fee:** often a small yearly fee.
- **Move-out costs:** cleaning and repair charges deducted from the deposit.

### 2.4 How to lower the initial cost

- Search for **"no key money" (礼金なし)** and **"no deposit" (敷金なし)** properties. Check the move-out cleaning and fee terms before you assume it is cheaper.
- Ask the agency about **free rent (フリーレント)** periods.
- Move at **off-peak times**. January-February and March are busiest; May-August and autumn are slower.
- Negotiate **before** you submit the application. See the email template in Appendix C.
- Consider UR (Urban Renaissance Agency) or other public housing, which generally charge no key money, agency fee, or guarantor fee. Check eligibility.

## Chapter 3: Can a Foreigner Rent?

### 3.1 The honest answer

Yes. Japanese law does not forbid foreigners from renting. In practice, some landlords refuse because of language concerns or fear of trouble. This is unfair but real. The fastest solution is to work with agencies that **say they accept foreign customers** and to search properties marked **"Foreigners OK" (外国人可)**.

### 3.2 What helps you get approved

- A stable income and a valid residence status
- A residence period that fits the contract term (a visa expiring in 3 months can be a problem)
- A Japanese-speaking emergency contact, if available
- A calm, polite, well-prepared application

### 3.3 Typical documents

| Document | Notes |
|---|---|
| Residence card (在留カード) | Front and back |
| Passport | |
| Proof of income | Employment certificate, pay slips, tax certificate, or an offer letter. Students may need a parent's income proof or a scholarship letter |
| Application form | Filled in Japanese; ask the agency for help |
| Emergency contact | A person living in Japan, if possible |
| Guarantor | Either a person or, more commonly, a guarantor company |
| Seal (印鑑) or signature | Many contracts accept a signature now. Ask. |

### 3.4 Guarantor vs guarantor company

- **Personal guarantor (連帯保証人):** a person, usually a Japanese resident relative or friend, who is liable if you do not pay. Hard for many foreigners to find.
- **Guarantor company (保証会社):** a paid service. Most foreigners use this. The company screens you, so a company that rejects you can mean the whole application fails. Ask the agency which company they use.

### 3.5 If you are rejected

Do not take it personally. Ask the agency to try another property or a different guarantor company. Agencies for foreign customers have different relationships with landlords, so try a second agency.

## Chapter 4: Finding a Place

### 4.1 Where to search

- **Large Japanese portals** (SUUMO, HOME'S, at home): the most listings. Use the "foreigners OK" filter.
- **Foreigner-friendly agencies:** often offer English support and handle landlords that accept foreigners.
- **Share houses and furnished/monthly apartments:** easy for short stays, no key money, but higher monthly cost.
- **Public housing (UR, prefectural, city):** low costs, no guarantor needed. Check the requirements.

### 4.2 Decide priorities

Rank these before you view anything: **rent limit, commute time, distance to the station (walking minutes), room size, building age, pet rules, and internet availability.** You will not find a perfect place. Pick your top three.

### 4.3 How to read a listing sheet (マイソク)

Key items to check:

- **Rent and management fee** (賃料, 管理費・共益費)
- **Deposit/key money** (敷金/礼金)
- **Walk to the station:** 80 meters = 1 minute. It does not count stairs, traffic lights, or hills.
- **Building age and structure** (築年数, 構造)
- **Direction (向き):** south-facing means more sun.
- **Contract type and term** (契約形態, 契約期間)
- **Move-in date** (入居可能日)
- **Conditions** (入居条件): "foreigners OK," "no pets," "single person only," "no office use," and so on.

## Chapter 5: Viewing and Applying

### 5.1 Viewing (内見, naiken)

Book by email or phone. Plan 3-4 properties on the same day if you can, and take photos (ask permission first). Use the checklist in Appendix D. Do not forget:

- Water pressure and hot water
- Mobile phone signal inside
- Noise from neighbors, road, or railway
- Garbage station and rules
- Washing machine space and balcony
- Mold, damp spots, and damage you should record

### 5.2 Applying (入居申込)

When you decide, you submit the **application form (入居申込書)** with your documents. In Japan this is usually not a binding contract, but it **does reserve the property** while the landlord screens you.

- Do not pay large sums at this step. A small holding amount is sometimes asked; ask whether it is **refundable** and get it in writing.
- Withdraw politely and quickly if you change your mind, **before** the contract, and **before** the documents go to the guarantor company.

### 5.3 Screening

Takes about 2-7 days. The landlord and the guarantor company check income and status. The agency may call your workplace or emergency contact. Answer phone calls and emails quickly.

## Chapter 6: The Contract

### 6.1 Explanation of important matters (重要事項説明)

Before you sign, a licensed agent must explain the key terms of the property and contract. This can sometimes be done online. **Ask for the document in advance** so you can read it or run it through a translator. Do not be embarrassed to ask questions.

### 6.2 Clauses to read carefully

| Clause | What to check |
|---|---|
| Contract period and renewal | 2 years? Renewal fee? Fixed term? |
| Rent, fees, payment date | Date and method; late fee |
| Termination notice | Usually **1 month**, sometimes 2. Must be in writing |
| Early termination penalty | Some contracts charge if you leave within 1-2 years |
| Restoration to original condition (原状回復) | Who pays for what at move-out |
| Prohibited actions | Pets, subletting, noise, smoking, extra occupants, business use |
| Guarantor/emergency contact | Who is responsible |
| Repair responsibility | What the tenant pays vs landlord |
| Special terms (特約) | Extra rules, such as mandatory cleaning fee. **Read these first** |

### 6.3 Signing and paying

- Sign or seal where indicated. Keep your own copy of everything.
- Pay initial costs by **bank transfer** before the deadline. Keep the receipt.
- Keys are handed over on or just before the move-in date.

### 6.4 Take a record

On the day you get the keys, photograph **every scratch, stain, or damage** and send them to the management company by email. This is your protection when you move out.

## Chapter 7: Move-in

### 7.1 Utilities

| Service | What to do |
|---|---|
| Electricity | Call or apply online; choose a provider |
| Water | Contact the city waterworks bureau, or use the postcard in the apartment |
| Gas | **Requires you to be present** to open the line. Book early (2+ weeks in busy season) |
| Internet | Check what the building supports. Installation can take 1-4 weeks |

### 7.2 Official registration

- **Report your new address within 14 days** of moving in, at your city or ward office. Bring your residence card. Late reports can technically lead to a fine, so do not put it off.
- **National Health Insurance / pension:** update if relevant.
- **Bank, employer, and school:** update your address.
- **Mail forwarding:** submit a change-of-address notice to Japan Post.

### 7.3 Garbage and neighbors

Garbage rules differ by city and sometimes by building. Ask the management company for the schedule and the correct bags. Greeting your neighbors briefly is welcome but not required in most urban apartments.

### 7.4 Basic rules to remember

- Keep noise low at night.
- Do not smoke on balconies unless allowed.
- Do not put out garbage on the wrong day. Complaints are common.
- Report leaks and breakdowns to the management company quickly.

## Chapter 8: Living and Moving Out

### 8.1 Renewal

Around 2-4 months before the end of the term, you receive renewal papers. Pay any renewal fee and send the signed forms back. You can also move out instead.

### 8.2 Giving notice to leave

- Send **written notice** following the contract (often 1 month, sometimes 2 months before leaving).
- Use the template in Appendix C. Keep proof.
- Ask for the **move-out inspection date**.

### 8.3 Move-out costs and deposit

When you leave, the landlord restores the room and deducts costs from your deposit. In general:

- **Normal wear and tear** (sun-faded wallpaper, furniture marks, aging) is usually the **landlord's** cost.
- **Damage you caused** (burns, deep scratches, mold from neglect, smoke or pet damage) can be charged to you.

The Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism publishes a guideline on restoration to original condition. If a bill seems too high, ask for an **itemized breakdown (明細)**.

### 8.4 If there is a problem

- Ask the management company for a written answer.
- Use local consumer centers and multilingual support (see Appendix H).

## Chapter 9: FAQ and Red Flags

**Do I need a Japanese guarantor?**
Usually not. A guarantor company often replaces one, but you may still need an emergency contact.

**Can I rent with only a few months left on my visa?**
It is harder. Some landlords prefer 1 year or more remaining. Ask the agency early.

**Can I rent before I arrive in Japan?**
Sometimes. Remote viewing and online contracts exist, but many agencies want you to be in Japan. Short-term furnished places are a safer first step.

**Is key money negotiable?**
Sometimes, especially for properties that have been empty for a while. Ask politely before applying.

**Can I have a pet or roommate?**
Only if the contract allows it. Unapproved extra occupants can break the contract.

**Red flags**

- Pressure to pay a large sum before you see a written contract
- Unexplained "support packages" or extra fees
- Refusal to provide documents in advance
- Price far below the area average with no clear reason
- Verbal promises that are not in the contract
- Being asked to send money by non-traceable methods

## Appendix C: Email Templates

*Tip: Send in English. Paste the Japanese version if you can have it checked. Keep sentences short.*

**C1. First inquiry**

> Subject: Inquiry about [Property name / Listing number]
>
> Hello,
>
> I found the property "[Property name]" on [site name]. I am interested in renting it.
> I am a [student / company employee] from [country], living in [Japan / my home country].
> My residence status is [status], valid until [date].
>
> 1. Is the property still available?
> 2. Do you accept foreign residents?
> 3. Could you tell me the total initial cost?
> 4. Which guarantor company do you use?
> 5. When can I view it?
>
> I can read basic Japanese, but English is easier for me. Thank you for your help.
>
> [Name] / [Phone] / [Email]

**C2. Request a viewing**

> Subject: Viewing request: [Property name]
>
> Hello,
> I would like to view [Property name]. Are you available on [date/time 1] or [date/time 2]?
> I would also like to view other properties near [area] if you have them.
> Thank you.

**C3. Negotiation (before applying)**

> Subject: Question about conditions: [Property name]
>
> Hello,
> I would like to apply for [Property name]. Before I do, may I ask whether the following are negotiable?
> - Reduce the key money (reikin) to [X] month(s) / waive it
> - Free rent for [X] month(s)
> - A reduced agency fee
>
> I plan to move in on [date] and can apply right away if we can agree on the conditions.
> Thank you for considering this.

**C4. Application**

> Subject: Rental application: [Property name]
>
> Hello,
> I would like to apply for [Property name], room [number]. I have attached the application form and the following documents: [list].
> Please let me know if anything else is needed and what the next steps are.

**C5. Notice of termination**

> Subject: Notice of termination: [Property name, Room number]
>
> Hello,
> I am writing to give notice that I will move out of [Property name, Room number] on [date]. This is in accordance with the contract, which requires [1 month] notice.
> Please let me know:
> 1. Any forms I must submit
> 2. The date and time of the move-out inspection
> 3. How my deposit will be refunded
>
> My forwarding address is [address]. Thank you.
> [Name / Room number / Phone]

**C6. Deposit refund inquiry**

> Subject: Deposit refund: [Property name, Room number]
>
> Hello,
> I moved out of [Property name, Room number] on [date]. I have not yet received the settlement of my deposit.
> Could you please send me an itemized statement of the restoration costs and the planned refund date?
> Thank you.
