# How Much Does It Cost to Rent an Apartment in Japan? (Initial Costs, 2026)

*Meta description (150 chars): What you really pay before you move into a rental in Japan: deposit, key money, agency fee, guarantor fee, insurance, and how to lower the total.*

You found an apartment you like. The rent is ¥80,000 a month. Then the agency sends the estimate, and the first bill is more than ¥400,000. Nothing is wrong. In Japan, the money you pay **before** you move in is usually about **4 to 6 times the monthly rent**. This article explains each item, what is fixed by law, what is negotiable, and how to lower the total.

*All amounts are typical ranges, not fixed rules. They change by city, landlord, and agency. Ask for a written breakdown before you apply.*

## The initial costs, item by item

| Item | Japanese | Typical amount | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| First month's rent | 前家賃 | 1 month | Often prorated if you move in mid-month |
| Security deposit | 敷金 (shikikin) | 0-2 months | Held by the landlord; the part left after repairs is returned at move-out |
| Key money | 礼金 (reikin) | 0-2 months | A gift to the landlord. **Not returned** |
| Agency fee | 仲介手数料 | Up to 0.5-1 month + tax | See the legal limit below |
| Guarantor company fee | 保証料 | 50-100% of 1 month | Many landlords require a guarantor company |
| Fire insurance | 火災保険 | About ¥15,000-20,000 for 2 years | Usually required |
| Key exchange | 鍵交換費用 | About ¥10,000-25,000 | Sometimes required |

## The one number set by law: the agency fee

The maximum that an agency can receive from the landlord and the tenant **together** is one month of rent plus tax. For housing, the tenant's default share is **half a month plus tax**. You pay up to a full month only if you agreed to it in advance. If a quote shows a full month charged to you and you do not remember agreeing, ask why. The amount comes from the ministry's [official notice on brokerage fees](https://www.mlit.go.jp/notice/noticedata/sgml/1970/26197000/26197000.html), issued under [Article 46 of the Real Estate Brokerage Act](https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/327AC1000000176).

## An example: a ¥80,000 apartment

Rent ¥80,000, management fee ¥5,000, deposit 1 month, key money 1 month, agency fee 1 month plus tax, guarantor fee 50%:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| First month rent + management fee | ¥85,000 |
| Agency fee (incl. tax) | ¥88,000 |
| Security deposit | ¥80,000 |
| Key money | ¥80,000 |
| Guarantor company fee | ¥42,500 |
| Fire insurance | ¥20,000 |
| Key exchange | ¥15,000 |
| **Total** | **¥410,500 (about 5.1 × the monthly rent)** |

If the agency charged only the default half month for the agency fee, the total would be lower by ¥44,000.

## Costs people forget

- **Renewal fee (更新料):** common in some regions, often one month's rent every two years. Not every property has it.
- **Guarantor renewal fee:** often a small yearly fee.
- **Move-out costs:** cleaning and repair charges, taken from the deposit. The ministry's [restoration guideline](https://www.mlit.go.jp/jutakukentiku/house/jutakukentiku_house_tk3_000020.html) says normal wear and aging are included in the rent, not charged to the tenant.
- **Monthly costs:** compare apartments by **rent plus management fee**, not rent alone.

## How to lower the initial cost

1. **Search for "no key money" (礼金なし) and "no deposit" (敷金なし) listings.** Check the cleaning and move-out terms before you assume it is cheaper.
2. **Ask about free rent (フリーレント).** Some landlords give the first month or two rent-free.
3. **Move at off-peak times.** January to March is the busiest. May to August and autumn are slower.
4. **Negotiate before you apply.** Key money is often negotiable, especially for rooms that have been empty for a while.
5. **Consider UR (Urban Renaissance Agency) housing.** It generally charges no key money, agency fee, or guarantor fee. Check the eligibility rules.

## Ask for these in writing

- A full breakdown of every initial cost
- The reason for any "support package" or "cleaning fee"
- Which items are refundable (the deposit) and which are not (key money)
