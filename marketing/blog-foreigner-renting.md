# Can a Foreigner Rent an Apartment in Japan? Documents and Guarantors

*Meta description (150 chars): Yes, foreigners can rent in Japan. Here are the documents agencies ask for, how guarantor companies work, and what to do if you are rejected.*

You find an apartment you like. You send an inquiry, and the reply asks for documents you have never heard of, plus a guarantor. It is easy to assume that the problem is your nationality.

It usually is not. Renting is not closed to foreigners. The practical difficulty is that some landlords are cautious about tenants they cannot easily check, and many agencies do not have an English process. The good news: with the right agency and a complete application, this is a normal, solvable process.

*This article is general information, not legal advice. Requirements differ by city, landlord, and agency. Confirm the details with the agent and your own contract.*

## What actually helps

Agencies and landlords are mostly checking for two things: can you pay, and will you stay.

- **A valid residence status and a visa that lasts long enough.** A visa expiring in three months can be a problem for a standard two-year contract. Ask early what term the property needs.
- **Stable income and proof of it.** An employment certificate, recent pay slips, a tax certificate, or an offer letter. Students are often asked for a parent's income proof or a scholarship letter.
- **A Japanese-speaking emergency contact**, if you can arrange one. This is a practical requirement, not a legal one, but it is asked for often.
- **A complete, polite application.** A filled-in application form and the documents below, sent promptly, make a real difference.

The fastest route is an agency that says it accepts foreign customers, and listings marked "foreigners OK" (外国人可). You will waste less time on properties that were never going to work.

## Documents you will usually be asked for

| Document | Notes |
|---|---|
| Residence card (在留カード) | Front and back ([official explanation](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/procedures/whatzairyu_00001.html)). Your address on the card is updated after you move in |
| Passport | Bring the original |
| Proof of income | See the options above |
| Application form | Usually in Japanese. Ask the agency for help before you guess at a field |
| Emergency contact | Ideally someone living in Japan |
| Guarantor or guarantor company | Almost always a company in practice |
| Seal (印鑑) or signature | Many contracts accept a signature now. Ask |

Requirements vary. Treat this as a checklist to confirm, not a fixed rule. After you move in, you have 14 days to report your new address to the city or ward office ([Resident Registration Act, Article 22](https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/342AC0000000081)).

## Guarantor vs guarantor company

A **personal guarantor (連帯保証人)** is a person — usually a Japanese resident relative or friend — who is liable if you do not pay. Many foreigners cannot find one.

A **guarantor company (保証会社)** is a paid service. It screens you and guarantees your rent to the landlord. This is the normal route for foreign tenants. The fee is typically in the range of **50–100% of one month's rent**, though it depends on the company.

The part people miss: the guarantor company screens you too. If one company rejects you, the whole application can fail even though the landlord had no objection. Ask the agency **which company they use** before you submit, and whether they can try another one.

## If you are rejected

It happens, and it is rarely the end of the search.

1. Ask the agency for the reason. Sometimes it is a document, sometimes the guarantor company.
2. Ask them to try a different guarantor company or another property.
3. Try a second agency that works with foreign tenants. Agencies have different relationships with landlords, so the same application can get a different answer.
4. If time is short, consider a share house, a furnished monthly apartment, or UR housing while you look for a longer-term place.

You do not need to accept the first answer as final. [FRESC (Foreign Residents Support Center)](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/support/fresc/fresc01.html) also publishes a list of [consultation desks](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/support/fresc/fresc_2.html) for foreign residents.

## Before you apply

Ask for these in writing:

- The full breakdown of initial costs, and which items are refundable
- Which guarantor company they use and what the fee is
- Whether the listing accepts foreign tenants
- What the contract term is, and what happens at renewal

A clear written answer is a good sign. Pressure to pay before you have seen a contract is not.
